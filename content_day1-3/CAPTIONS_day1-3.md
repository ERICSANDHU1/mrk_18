I was wrong about what makes AI hard.

For a year I thought the model was the hard part. Pick the smartest one, prompt it well, done.

### ERIC — personal · `Day-02/d2_eric.png`
**Caption:**
Most things being called "AI agents" are just chatbots.

Quick mental model I keep coming back to:

A chatbot answers. You ask, it replies, the loop ends with you.

An agent acts. It runs its own loop — look at the goal, decide a step, use a tool, check the result, repeat, until the goal is done.

The difference isn't intelligence. It's autonomy plus a feedback loop.

A model that writes you an email = assistant. A system that decides the email should go out, sends it, and checks whether it bounced = agent.

No loop and no action? It's smarter autocomplete — which is fine. Just don't call it an agent.

What's the most genuinely "agentic" thing you've seen actually ship — not a demo, real usage?

### ERIC — personal · `Day-03/d3_eric.png` (carousel cover / infographic)
**Caption:**
An AI agent is a model wrapped in a harness. The model is the easy part.

I drew the map I wish I'd had a year ago.

In the middle: the model — the reasoning core. Swappable.

Around it, the harness — the part you actually engineer:
• Guardrails — what it's allowed to do
• Skills / playbooks — repeatable procedures
• Tools — live links to data and actions
• Memory — so it doesn't start from zero
• Evaluators — it scores its own output and retries
• Orchestration — routing work, deciding human vs auto

Swap the model and most of your system survives. Remove the harness and you just have a chatbot.

The moat was never the model.

Which layer of the harness do you think is the most underrated?
