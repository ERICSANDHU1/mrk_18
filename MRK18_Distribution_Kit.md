## THE ONE-PARAGRAPH STRATEGY (read this first)

You have **two** distribution jobs, not one — and confusing them is why this felt murky.

1. **First 10 paying design partners (₹4,999/mo).** This is *hand-to-hand sales*. You close these in DMs. The waitlist does almost nothing here.
2. **Everyone after that (path to 10,000).** This is where the waitlist earns its keep — as a **ranked lead pipeline + social-proof counter + content fuel**, not a "coming soon" page.

**Sequencing:**
- **Now → 22 Jun:** Waitlist live and quietly climbing from your build-in-public posts. You spend these days hand-picking and DMing 5 named design partners. No big public launch on the 22nd.
- **22 Jun → ~6 Jul:** Close DP1–DP10 by hand (~50 personal touches/day, founder = the closer). Every signed partner becomes a build-in-public post.
- **~6 Jul onward:** Open the waitlist in **batches of 20–30**, not all at once. Protects a 2-person team, keeps onboarding quality high, manufactures urgency.

**The Virtual Device leads the story — as the HOOK, not the product you sell.** The VD ("a CMO in your pocket" — records video + voice, analyzes in real time) is a *working demo*, not production. So split the two jobs cleanly:

- **The VD demo earns the click.** A 30-second clip of the device watching a real campaign and *speaking* the bitter truth out loud is a scroll-stopper nobody else in India has. It leads every post, the waitlist, and the DM.
- **The watchdog software earns the card.** It's the part every founder can actually use on 22 Jun. The demo makes them *want in*; the watchdog is what they *get in to*.

**The one rule that protects you: show the demo, sell the access.** Never let a paying founder believe a physical device ships to them on 22 Jun. Frame it as a *queue*, not a delivery: *"The CMO in your pocket is real — watch it work. We're putting it in founders' hands in waves. Get on the list and you're in line for the device; start with the watchdog today."* "In line for the device" = honest *and* a stronger reason to hold a waitlist spot than software access alone.

# PART 2 — DESIGN-PARTNER OUTREACH KIT

Goal: close **5–10 named founders at ₹4,999/mo, charged from day one.** You are the AE. ~50 personal touches/day. Give one real teardown *for free* in the DM — that's the hook that earns the call.

## Step 0 — Build your hit list (do this first)
Pick **20–30 named Indian founders** you can reach (not "Indian SaaS" in the abstract — actual humans with a name and a handle). Sources: your build-in-public audience, your network, founders posting their ad screenshots on LinkedIn/X, small D2C/SaaS founders running their own marketing. Rank by: (a) running paid ads now, (b) active on social so you have a public signal to tear down, (c) reachable warmly.

## The opening DM — lead with the demo video
> Hey [Name] — saw your [post / launch / ad for X]. I built a device that watches founders' campaigns and tells them the bitter truth *out loud*. Here's 30 seconds of it reading a real one: [VD demo clip]
>
> I pointed an early version at your last campaign and it flagged one uncomfortable thing: **[ONE specific, real observation** — e.g. "your CTA sends paid traffic to a page with 3 competing asks, so clicks are up but signups aren't moving"].
>
> Not selling anything here — want the full 3-point teardown? Takes me 5 min.

**Why it works:** the video stops the scroll in a way text can't, *then* you name something real and ask for nothing. The clip earns the open; the free teardown is the door. Once they say "yes, send it," you're in a conversation, not a pitch.

> **Note:** the DM shows the device demo, but what you actually sell them is watchdog access today + their place in line for the device. Never imply hardware ships to them on signup.

## The follow-up after they say yes → the offer
> Here's the full teardown: [3 bullet points — what's working, what's leaking, the one move].
>
> This is exactly what MRK18 does every week, automatically — reads your channels, calls you with the bitter truth, hands you the 3 fixes. I'm taking on **7–10 founding design partners at ₹4,999/mo**, charged from day one (real skin in the game keeps me honest). You'd be one of the first 10 in India. Want in?

## The 4-step email sequence (for warm leads who didn't close in DM)

**Email 1 — Day 0 · The teardown (no ask)**
Subject: *The bitter truth about [Company]'s last campaign*
Body: The same 3-point teardown, in writing. Close with: "No ask here. If it's useful, reply and I'll show you what running this every week looks like."

**Email 2 — Day 2 · The mechanism**
Subject: *Why your dashboard shows green while your bank doesn't*
Body: Explain the watchdog → adviser → approval-gated flow. One line: "It reads past the green arrows so you don't have to learn to." Soft CTA: "Want to be one of our first 10 design partners?"

**Email 3 — Day 5 · The proof + scarcity**
Subject: *3 founders in — 7 founding spots left*
Body: Share what an early DP saw on their first call (with permission). Scarcity is real: cap is 7–10. "Card on file from day one — that's how I know it's working for you, not just interesting."

**Email 4 — Day 9 · The close / breakup**
Subject: *Closing the founding cohort*
Body: "Locking the founding-partner list this week. ₹4,999/mo, founding pricing held for life. After this it's ₹9,999 at public launch. In or out — either is fine, just don't want you to miss it." Last line makes the no-pressure exit explicit; that's what makes people say yes.

> **Rule:** every DP who signs becomes a build-in-public post within 24h ("DP #3 just got their first bitter-truth call — here's what it told them"). That post recruits the next DP *and* climbs your waitlist counter. The two engines feed each other.
