## 0. How to read this document

This PRD covers **only the Execution Workflow** — the "doing" half of mrk18 that begins *after* a founder onboards and the system has analyzed their inputs. It does not cover billing, the marketing site, or the analytics/reporting product beyond what the workflow needs.

Two conventions used throughout:

- **[AUTO]** — fully automated by the system in V1.
- **[ASSISTED]** — automated *draft/recommendation*, but a human (founder) or the mrk18 backend team must approve or complete a step before it goes live.
- **[MANUAL / BACKEND]** — done by the mrk18 backend/ops team (or the founder) by hand in V1; targeted for automation later.

Every functional requirement is tagged so engineering and the backend team know exactly who owns each step.

## 1. Overview & problem statement

Indian startup founders are expected to be their own CMO — strategy, copy, design, posting, and ads — while building the actual product. Most have no marketing team, a tight budget (costs reasoned in INR, not USD), and need to show up consistently across LinkedIn, X, and Instagram in English *and* Indic languages.

mrk18 is an **AI CMO**: the founder describes their business once, and the system continuously produces and publishes on-brand marketing content. The **Execution Workflow** is the engine that converts a founder's brand brief and the system's analysis into **published, approved content** with the founder in control at the moment that matters (before anything goes live).

The workflow is deliberately built as a **structured multi-agent pipeline with a human approval gate**, not an autonomous "fire-and-forget" swarm. This is a product decision as much as a technical one: for a founder's brand, trust comes from *reviewing before publishing*, and content pipelines have tight step-to-step dependencies that favor a predictable workflow over autonomous agents (see §6 and Appendix A).

### 1.1 What V1 delivers

A founder can register, complete a brand intake, and receive a **content kit** (posts + images, with reels assisted) generated by a coordinated set of AI agents. The founder reviews and approves in one screen. Approved content auto-publishes to **LinkedIn, X, and Instagram**. **Meta Ads** is drafted by the system but launched with a human in the loop.

### 1.2 What V1 explicitly does NOT deliver (non-goals)

- No fully autonomous ad spend / auto-launch of paid campaigns. Ads are **draft-and-recommend** only (§7.4).
- No free-form, one-click text-to-reel video. Reels are **template/avatar-assisted** with a human check (§7.3.3).
- No publishing to platforms beyond LinkedIn, X, Instagram (no YouTube, no WhatsApp broadcast, no email in V1).
- No multi-user / agency seats, approval chains, or role hierarchies — single founder account in V1.
- No closed-loop performance optimization (the system reports, it does not auto-tune campaigns in V1).
- No on-device / offline generation.

## 5. Stage 1 — Registration & input collection

### 5.1 Purpose
Capture everything the agents need to produce on-brand content, and connect the publishing channels — with the fewest possible steps, because every step is a drop-off risk for a busy founder.

### 5.2 Sub-flows

**5.2.1 Account registration [AUTO]**
- Email + password or Google OAuth sign-up. Single founder account (no teams in V1).
- Lightweight email verification.

**5.2.2 Brand intake — the Brand Brief [AUTO, guided]**
A structured, conversational intake that produces a typed `BrandBrief`. Fields:

- Business: name, one-line description, category/vertical, website/URL.
- Audience: who they sell to, geography (incl. Tier-1/2/3 if relevant), languages wanted (English + selected Indic languages).
- Voice & brand: tone (e.g. bold, friendly, expert), do's and don'ts, words/claims to avoid, sample of existing copy if any.
- Goals: primary objective (awareness, signups, hiring, fundraising), target platforms, posting cadence.
- Assets: logo, brand colors, product screenshots (optional upload).
- Compliance/constraints: regulated claims, competitor names to avoid, etc.

The intake should pre-fill sensibly (e.g. infer category from the website) and let the founder correct. Output is validated against a Pydantic schema before it can advance (§6.4).

**5.2.3 Channel connection [ASSISTED / BACKEND-gated]**
Founder connects LinkedIn, X, Instagram, and (optionally) a Meta Ads account via OAuth. **Important platform reality (see Appendix C):** each platform requires a one-time app-review / verification gate that mrk18 (not the founder) must clear at the app level:

- **LinkedIn company-Page posting** needs Community Management API approval (~1–4 weeks, manual review). **[MANUAL / BACKEND]** to obtain; per-founder connection is [AUTO] once granted.
- **Instagram** publishing needs Meta App Review + Business Verification, and the founder must have an Instagram **Business** account (Creator accounts cannot publish Reels via API) linked to a Facebook Page. **[MANUAL / BACKEND]** for app review; founder connection is [ASSISTED] (we must guide them through Business-account + Page-link + Page Publishing Authorization).
- **X** requires a paid/metered API tier; the Free tier (17 posts/day) is unusable for production. **[MANUAL / BACKEND]** to set up the paid app; posting is [AUTO] thereafter (metered cost — §9).
- **Meta Ads** requires App Review + Business Verification + advanced `ads_management` access, with billing on the founder's own ad account. **[MANUAL / BACKEND]** + [ASSISTED].

> **Build note:** Because these app-review gates take weeks, treat them as a **pre-launch backend workstream**, not a per-user runtime step. The product should degrade gracefully — a founder whose Instagram isn't yet connected can still generate and approve content and export it for manual posting.

### 5.3 Functional requirements (Stage 1)

| ID | Requirement | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| S1-1 | User can register and verify email | [AUTO] |
| S1-2 | Guided Brand Brief intake producing a validated `BrandBrief` object | [AUTO] |
| S1-3 | Intake pre-fills from website where possible | [AUTO] |
| S1-4 | OAuth connect for LinkedIn / X / Instagram / Meta Ads | [AUTO] (post app-review) |
| S1-5 | Obtain & maintain platform app-review/verification approvals | [MANUAL / BACKEND] |
| S1-6 | Guide founder through Instagram Business-account + Page link + PPA | [ASSISTED] |
| S1-7 | Graceful fallback: generate + approve + **export** content if a channel isn't connected | [AUTO] |

## 6. Stage 2 & 3 — Analysis layer + Multi-agent orchestration

This is the heart of the Execution Workflow. **Recommended concrete stack (see Appendix A for the full evaluation):**

- **Orchestrator:** **LangGraph** — a `StateGraph` over a single typed state object, chosen specifically because it has best-in-class **human-in-the-loop interrupts** + **durable checkpointing** (pause for minutes/days, resume exactly where it stopped). This is the load-bearing capability our approval gate needs; CrewAI lacks built-in checkpointing for long-running human-gated flows, which disqualifies it for V1.
- **State contract:** **Pydantic** typed models flowing down the chain; structured outputs enforced between every node.
- **Model layer (provider-agnostic behind an abstraction):**
  - English long-form / brand voice / structured generation → a cheap-but-strong frontier model (e.g. Gemini 3 Flash class) as workhorse, premium model reserved for hard cases; use prompt caching.
  - Indic / regional / Hinglish copy → **Sarvam** (Sarvam-30B / 105B; Apache-2.0, India-sovereign, currently low/zero API cost) — also our "Made-in-India" / IndiaAI-Mission narrative.
  - Localization → AI4Bharat **IndicTrans2** (free, 22 languages) for English→regional.
  - Images → FLUX.1 [schnell] (Apache-2.0) / SD 3.5 for volume; **GPT Image 2 / Imagen 4** for text-heavy Indic-script creatives (only these reliably render Devanagari).
  - Reels → templated avatar (HeyGen / TrueFan for Indic lip-sync) + Kling/Veo b-roll — **assisted, not one-click** in V1.
- **Publishing layer:** **Ayrshare** (or equivalent) for LinkedIn/X/Instagram, with its native `requiresApproval` + idempotency support mirroring our gate.
- **Observability:** **LangSmith** (or OpenTelemetry GenAI traces) — every node instrumented as a span from day one; small (~20-case) LLM-as-judge eval set.

### 6.1 Analysis layer (Stage 2) [AUTO]
A deterministic + LLM step that turns the `BrandBrief` (plus light market/competitor/audience signals) into a `StrategyPlan`: content angles, hooks, post count, per-platform plan, recommended languages. This is the input to the generation agents. Kept lightweight in V1 (no heavy market-research swarm).

### 6.2 Agent roster (Stage 3)

The pipeline is a **sequential backbone + one reflection (critic) loop + one human interrupt** — the right shape for a known, ordered content task.

```
[Intake/Normalizer] → Strategist → Copywriter ⇄ Reviewer(critic, max 2 loops)
                    → Designer ⇄ Reviewer(visual check)
                    → Assembler → ⛔ HUMAN APPROVAL → Publisher → Done
```

| Agent | Reads | Writes | Model tier | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Intake/Normalizer** (deterministic) | raw brief form | validated `BrandBrief` | — | Guardrail: reject off-policy / prompt-injection here |
| **Strategist** | `brief` | `strategy` (angles, hooks, #posts, per-platform plan) | strong | explicit objective + output schema |
| **Copywriter** | `brief`, `strategy` | `copy[]` (text/CTA/hashtags per platform & language) | strong (Indic → Sarvam) | generator in the reflection loop |
| **Reviewer (Critic)** | `brief`, `copy[]`, `visuals[]` | `review` (pass/fail + per-item feedback + score) | cheap/fast | separate persona to avoid "yes-man" bias; on fail → route back; cap 2 iterations |
| **Designer** | `brief`, `strategy`, approved `copy[]` | `visuals[]` (image/reel specs + asset URLs) | image/video models | may generate N variants in parallel; store assets in object storage, pass **URLs** in state |
| **Assembler** (deterministic) | `copy[]`, `visuals[]` | platform-ready post objects | — | builds the publisher payload |
| **⛔ Human gate** (interrupt) | full state | `approval` | — | LangGraph `interrupt` before publish; approve/edit/reject |
| **Publisher** | approved posts, `approval` | `publish_results[]` | — | Ayrshare `/post` with idempotency key + schedule |

### 6.3 Shared state contract (the content brief that flows down the chain)

```
CampaignState:
  brief:            BrandBrief        # input
  strategy:         StrategyPlan      # strategist
  copy:             list[PostCopy]    # copywriter (per post/platform/language)
  visuals:          list[MediaAsset]  # designer (asset URLs, not blobs)
  review:           ReviewResult      # reviewer (pass/fail + feedback + score)
  approval:         ApprovalDecision  # HUMAN (approved/edited/rejected + notes)
  publish_results:  list[PublishResult]
  meta:             RunMeta           # iteration counts, token/cost, trace_id
```

Handoffs must be **explicit** (objective + output format + boundaries per agent) — vague handoffs cause agents to duplicate or misinterpret work. Large artifacts (images/reels) are stored in object storage (S3/GCS); only URLs travel in state.

### 6.4 Reliability requirements [AUTO]

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| R-1 | Structured-output validation (Pydantic/JSON-Schema) between **every** node; a node only proceeds if the prior artifact validates |
| R-2 | Pre-LLM input guardrail (prompt-injection, off-brand/off-policy) using a cheap model before expensive generation |
| R-3 | Post-generation guardrails: brand-safety, toxicity, claim/compliance check against brief's do-not list |
| R-4 | Reflection loop capped (max 2 iterations) to bound cost/latency |
| R-5 | Retries with deterministic checkpoints; idempotency key on publish so retries never double-post |
| R-6 | Model routing: cheap/fast model for routing/guardrails/critic first-pass; strong model only for generation |
| R-7 | Full tracing (span per node, stable trace IDs across retries) + small LLM-as-judge eval set from day one |
| R-8 | Durable checkpointer (Postgres in prod) so a run can sit paused at the approval gate for days |

## 7. Stage 4–6 — Approval, Publishing, Reporting

### 7.1 Stage 4 — Human approval gate [ASSISTED — the core trust feature]

- The LangGraph run **interrupts before the Publisher node**; full state is serialized to the checkpointer and the run can wait indefinitely.
- The founder sees one **review screen**: every generated post with its copy, language, target platform, and attached visual.
- Per item, the founder can **Approve / Edit / Reject**:
  - *Edit* → tweak caption/hashtags/visual choice inline before publish.
  - *Reject (with note)* → routes feedback back into the reflection loop for regeneration.
- Founder sets/confirms **schedule** (publish now or at a chosen time) and per-platform targeting.
- On submit, the run resumes on the same `thread_id` with the approval decisions.
- **Defense-in-depth:** the gate is enforced in *both* the orchestrator (interrupt) and the publisher (Ayrshare `requiresApproval`), so nothing can publish un-approved.

| ID | Requirement | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| S4-1 | Run pauses before publish and persists full state | [AUTO] |
| S4-2 | Single review screen with per-item approve/edit/reject | [ASSISTED] |
| S4-3 | Edits and rejections feed back correctly (regeneration on reject) | [AUTO] |
| S4-4 | Founder sets schedule + per-platform target | [ASSISTED] |
| S4-5 | No content publishes without an explicit approval decision | [AUTO] guard |

### 7.2 Stage 5 — Organic publishing [AUTO]

Publishing of **approved** content to LinkedIn, X, Instagram via the publishing layer. Backend owns scheduling and rate-limit enforcement (platform APIs publish "now"; mrk18 holds the schedule).

Per-platform reality (full detail + sources in Appendix C):

| Platform | Channel | V1 status | Key constraints the backend must handle |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Personal profile | **[AUTO]** | Undocumented per-app rate limits → backoff |
| LinkedIn | Company Page | **[AUTO]** after Community Mgmt API review (~1–4 wks) | ~150 uploads/24hr/account; rejection risk on review |
| X | Posts + media | **[AUTO]**, metered | Paid tier required (~$0.01/post pay-per-use); Free tier unusable; v2 chunked media upload |
| Instagram | Feed / Carousel | **[AUTO]** after Meta App Review + Biz Verification | JPEG-only, public-URL media, **no native scheduling**, 100 posts/24hr, carousel ≤10 items |
| Instagram | Reels | **[AUTO]** after review, **Business account only** | ≤90s via API; Creator accounts unsupported |
| Instagram | Stories | **[AUTO]** after review | media_type=STORIES |

| ID | Requirement | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| S5-1 | Publish approved posts to connected LinkedIn/X/Instagram | [AUTO] |
| S5-2 | Backend-managed scheduler honoring per-platform rate limits | [AUTO] |
| S5-3 | Idempotent publish (no double-posts on retry) | [AUTO] |
| S5-4 | Per-post publish status + failure surfaced to founder; retry/backoff | [AUTO] |
| S5-5 | Webhook/status polling to confirm async media processing (esp. video) | [AUTO] |

### 7.3 Content generation specifics

**7.3.1 Posts/copy [AUTO]** — per platform + language; English via frontier model, Indic via Sarvam, localization via IndicTrans2.

**7.3.2 Images [AUTO]** — FLUX.1 [schnell]/SD 3.5 for base creatives; route **text-heavy / Indic-script** creatives to GPT Image 2 / Imagen 4 (only these render Devanagari reliably). Watch SD 3.5's "free under $1M revenue" threshold and avoid FLUX [dev] (non-commercial) for shipped content.

**7.3.3 Reels [ASSISTED]** — V1 is **template-first**: avatar (HeyGen / TrueFan for Indic Hinglish lip-sync) + captions + stock motion, with a mandatory human check. Free-form text→reel is **not production-ready** and is out of scope for one-click in V1.

### 7.4 Meta Ads — partial automation [ASSISTED / MANUAL-BACKEND]

This is the one channel that genuinely resists end-to-end automation. The barrier is not the API call — it is **business verification, billing liability, per-ad policy review, and Meta silently overriding settings (Advantage+)**. See Appendix C.4.

**What V1 automates vs. what stays human:**

| Step | V1 handling |
|---|---|
| Generate ad creative + copy + audience/budget recommendations | **[AUTO]** |
| Build Campaign / Ad Set / Ad objects as **PAUSED drafts** in the founder's verified ad account | **[ASSISTED]** (API draft) |
| Review budget, targeting, creative; **flip to ACTIVE / go live** | **[MANUAL / BACKEND or founder]** — human gate on the money |
| Monitor & report performance (insights are freely readable) | **[AUTO]** |
| Material budget / targeting changes | **[MANUAL / BACKEND]** — routed through human approval, never autonomous |

> **PRD-critical statement:** mrk18 V1 **does not** promise autonomous ad launching or autonomous spend. It automates the *labor* (creation, structuring, recommendations, reporting) and keeps a **human gate on go-live and budget**. This is both a technical necessity (access tiers, verification, quota) and a financial-safety decision.

### 7.5 Stage 6 — Reporting [AUTO, read-only]
Pull post + ad insights and present simple per-post / per-channel performance. **Read-only in V1** — the system reports and *recommends*, it does not auto-optimize campaigns.

## 8. Data model (core objects)

- `User` — auth, plan, connected channels (tokens stored encrypted), app-review status per channel.
- `BrandBrief` — see §5.2.2; the durable source of truth for voice/audience/constraints.
- `CampaignRun` — one execution of the pipeline; holds `CampaignState`, `thread_id`, status (`generating` / `awaiting_approval` / `publishing` / `done` / `failed`), cost & token meta.
- `ContentItem` — a single post: platform, language, copy, media asset URL(s), approval status, schedule, publish result.
- `MediaAsset` — generated image/reel; stored in object storage; URL + metadata + license/source model.
- `AdDraft` — drafted Meta Ads objects, status (`draft` / `pending_human` / `live`), budget, targeting.
- `PublishResult` — platform, external post ID, status, timestamps, errors.

Tokens and PII encrypted at rest; per-tenant isolation; India data-residency considered for sovereign-model self-hosting path.

## 9. Non-functional requirements

- **Performance:** finished intake → kit ready < 10 min (§2). Approval gate can persist for days without resource leak.
- **Cost control (INR-sensitive):** track fully-loaded cost per `CampaignRun` (tokens + image + video + publish-meter). Levers: sequential pipeline (not a swarm), capped reflection loops, model routing, prompt caching, Sarvam's low-cost Indic tier. Treat **X posting as a metered cost line**, not a flat fee. Re-verify all model/API prices at build time — pricing and "free tiers" (Sarvam free API, X pay-per-use, etc.) are volatile.
- **Reliability/availability:** ≥98% first-attempt publish success (excl. platform outages); graceful degradation if a channel is unconnected (export fallback).
- **Security/privacy:** encrypted token storage, per-tenant isolation, least-privilege OAuth scopes, audit log of every publish and every approval decision.
- **Observability:** span-level tracing on every agent node; cost & token dashboards; alert on guardrail failures and publish failures.
- **Compliance:** respect each platform's automation/policy terms; founder's do-not-claim list enforced by guardrails.

## 10. Risks & mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Platform app-review gates take weeks (LinkedIn Page, IG, Meta Ads) | Blocks launch / channels | Treat as **pre-launch backend workstream**; ship export-fallback so product works pre-approval |
| Generic / off-brand AI copy | Churn (founders hate "AI slop") | Strong brand-brief, reflection critic loop, edit-in-review, ≥70% no-edit-approval metric |
| Indic copy quality / script rendering | Weak Tier-2/3 value prop | Sarvam for Indic copy, IndicTrans2 for localization, GPT Image 2/Imagen 4 for Devanagari text-in-image |
| Cost blowout (multi-agent token burn, video) | Bad unit economics | Sequential pipeline, capped loops, model routing, caching; per-run cost tracking; reels assisted/templated |
| Meta Ads auto-spend bug burns founder budget | Financial + trust damage | **Human gate on go-live & budget** — no autonomous spend in V1 |
| Volatile model/API pricing & free tiers ending | Margin erosion | Provider-agnostic model abstraction; re-verify pricing at build; model unit economics on paid (not free) assumptions |
| Double-posting on retries | Embarrassing/trust | Idempotency keys; defense-in-depth approval enforced in orchestrator + publisher |
| Telephone effect across agent handoffs | Quality loss | Explicit typed handoffs; artifacts by reference (URLs); structured-output validation between nodes |

## Appendix A — Multi-agent architecture research (how it's actually done)

**Bottom line:** the mrk18 pipeline is a **deterministic sequential workflow with a reflection/critic loop and a human approval gate** — *not* an autonomous orchestrator-worker "swarm." Autonomous multi-agent swarms (e.g. Anthropic's research system: lead agent spawns parallel subagents) suit **open-ended, unpredictable, parallel** tasks and burn ~15× the tokens of a chat. A content pipeline has **known, ordered steps with tight dependencies**, which Anthropic explicitly notes is *not* a good fit for autonomous multi-agent systems. So we favor a structured workflow.

### A.1 Orchestration patterns evaluated

| Pattern | What it is | Fit for mrk18 |
|---|---|---|
| Orchestrator-worker (lead + subagents) | Lead decomposes & spawns parallel subagents | **Low** overall; use only *inside* the Designer to generate image variants in parallel |
| **Sequential pipeline** | Agents run in fixed order, output feeds next | **High — the backbone** (strategist→copywriter→designer→reviewer→publisher) |
| Hierarchical | Manager LLM dynamically delegates | Medium; overhead not worth it in V1 |
| Blackboard / shared-state | Specialists read/write shared structure | Low for autonomy, but our typed shared state is a lightweight version of this |
| **Reflection / critic loop (Reflexion)** | Generator drafts → separate Critic reviews → loop on fail | **High — wrap copywriter+reviewer**; separate critic persona beats self-critique (avoids yes-man bias); cap iterations |

### A.2 Framework comparison

| Framework | Core abstraction | Human-in-the-loop | State / checkpointing | Maturity (mid-2026) | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LangGraph (recommended)** | Graph / StateGraph over typed shared state | **Best in class** — interrupt before/after any node; resume approve/edit/reject | **Built-in checkpointers** (InMemory→SQLite→Postgres); durable resume; time-travel | Mature (0.4+, LangChain 1.0) | Steeper learning curve; you think in graphs |
| CrewAI | Role/Crew; sequential/hierarchical; Flows | Task-level human input only | **No built-in checkpointing** for long-running flows | Mature, high adoption | Fastest to prototype; **weakest on durable human-gated state → disqualifying for our gate** |
| OpenAI Agents SDK | Conversation + Handoffs | Built-in but lighter | Sessions; ephemeral context by default | Production-ready, tracing on by default | Clean API but OpenAI-centric, lighter persistence |
| Microsoft Agent Framework (AutoGen + Semantic Kernel) | Conversation + graph workflows | Yes | Session-based state | 1.0 only April 2026; heavier/.NET-leaning | Newest 1.0, enterprise-oriented |

**Why LangGraph for V1:** the single most important capability for a human-gated content pipeline is *pause safely → wait for a human → resume from exactly where it stopped*. LangGraph is the only option with this as a first-class, documented primitive backed by durable checkpointing. Start on `InMemorySaver` (dev) → ship on `AsyncPostgresSaver`. Pair with Pydantic (reported ~94% structured-output error reduction) and LangChain 1.0 middleware (retries, guardrails, prebuilt HumanInTheLoop).

### A.3 Reliability practices (folded into §6.4)
Structured-output validation between every node; pre/post guardrails with a cheap model; retries + checkpoints + idempotency; model routing for cost; full OpenTelemetry/LangSmith tracing with stable trace IDs; small (~20-case) LLM-as-judge eval set from day one. Token usage explains the majority of cost variance, so a sequential (not swarm) design with capped loops is the main cost lever.

## Appendix B — Model stack research (LLMs, images, video)

### B.1 Copy (text) — two-tier router
- **English / brand voice / structured generation → frontier, cheap tier:** Gemini 3 Flash-class workhorse (~$0.50/$3 per 1M tok in/out), premium (Claude Sonnet 4.6 / GPT-5.x) reserved for hard cases; prompt caching cuts ~90%.
- **Indic / regional / Hinglish → Sarvam** (Sarvam-30B ~32B/2.4B-active MoE, 65K ctx, Apache-2.0; Sarvam-105B ~106B/10B-active, 128K ctx, India's first fully domestically-trained open-source LLM, Apache-2.0). Currently free/near-free on Sarvam's first-party API (promotional — model unit economics on it ending). Self-host route for data residency + sovereignty narrative.
- **Localization → AI4Bharat IndicTrans2** (free, all 22 scheduled Indic languages).
- Secondary Indic option: **Ola Krutrim-2** (12B, 22 Indic languages). Track **BharatGen** (IIT-Bombay-led, largest IndiaAI-Mission allocation ~₹988.6 cr; Param2 17B MoE) for deeper sovereign/grant alignment.

**Where Indian models win:** Indic-language nuance + Hinglish, cost, data residency/sovereignty positioning (a real sales + IndiaAI-grant angle). **Where frontier still wins:** English long-form persuasion, instruction-following, tool-use/structured output, reliability — keep the model layer **provider-agnostic**.

### B.2 Images
- Base/volume → **FLUX.1 [schnell]** (Apache-2.0, ~cents/image) or **SD 3.5** (free for orgs <$1M revenue). Avoid **FLUX.1 [dev]** (non-commercial) for shipped content.
- Text-heavy / Indic-script creatives → **GPT Image 2** (first to reliably render Devanagari + Indic scripts) or **Imagen 4** (leads text accuracy/speed). This split is the key Indic-creative unlock.

### B.3 Reels / short video (V1 = templated, human-in-loop)
- Avatar/talking-head (most automatable): **HeyGen** (175+ languages) or **TrueFan** (strong Dravidian/Hinglish lip-sync, India-centric).
- B-roll/generative: **Kling 3.0** (~$0.10/sec, best value) for volume; **Veo 3.1** (best lip-sync, native 4K) when quality matters; Runway Gen-4.5 for hero content.
- **Do not build on Sora** (OpenAI discontinued it: app ended Apr 2026, API ends Sep 2026).
- Honest read: fully-automated brand-safe text→reel is the **roughest** part of the stack — keep reels assisted/templated in V1.

## Appendix C — Platform publishing & Meta Ads research (automated vs manual)

**Cross-cutting reality:** LinkedIn, Instagram, and (for a metered price) X allow **clean programmatic publishing of pre-approved organic content** after a one-time app-review/cost gate. **Meta Ads genuinely resists end-to-end automation** and needs a human/backend-assisted step in V1. The friction is the **app-review / business-verification gate** and, for ads, **billing + policy review + budget liability** — not the API call.

### C.1 LinkedIn
Community Management API (`/rest/posts`); same endpoint for profile vs Page, differing by author URN + scope (`w_member_social` vs `w_organization_social`). **Company-Page posting requires Community Management API approval — manual review, ~1–4 weeks, rejection possible.** Full media support (images, video ≤5GB/10min, document carousels). Rate limits undocumented per-app (one figure: ~150 uploads/24hr/account) → design for backoff. **Verdict: AUTOMATED after a multi-week review gate.**

### C.2 X (Twitter)
`POST /2/tweets` (v2); v2 chunked media upload (INIT→APPEND→FINALIZE→STATUS). **Pricing shifted: new developers default to pay-per-use (~$0.01/post)**; legacy Basic ($200/mo) / Pro ($5,000/mo) closed to new signups; Free tier (17 posts/24hr) is a sandbox, unusable for production. **Verdict: AUTOMATED but metered/paid** — budget X as a per-post cost line.

### C.3 Instagram
Content Publishing API: create container (`POST /<IG_ID>/media`) → publish (`POST /<IG_ID>/media_publish`). Requires an IG **professional** account; **Reels require a Business account (Creator unsupported)**; account linked to a Facebook Page; new scopes since the Jan 27 2025 deprecation; Page Publishing Authorization may block until completed; media must be at a **public URL** (JPEG-only images). Publishes feed/carousel(≤10)/Reels(≤90s via API)/Stories. **Rate limit 100 published posts/24hr/account; no native scheduling** (backend must hold schedule); needs Meta App Review + Business Verification. **Verdict: AUTOMATED after review; backend owns scheduling + rate limits.**

### C.4 Meta Ads
Marketing API can create/update/pause Campaigns/Ad Sets/Ads (`ads_management`, `ads_read`, `business_management`). **Why full automation is hard:** (1) tiered access wall — Standard/Marketing-API tier is limited until App Review + thresholds; `ads_management` advanced access is a second review round; (2) **mandatory Business Verification** (legal docs; each tenant's ad account under their own verified Business Manager); (3) **billing & budget liability** sits on the tenant's account — a bug burns real money; (4) **per-ad policy review** with human-judgment appeals; (5) **silent Advantage+ overrides** of targeting/placement; (6) tight quotas; (7) review timelines of days-to-weeks. **"Partial automation" means:** AI drafts creatives/copy/audiences/budget recommendations and builds **PAUSED draft** campaigns via API; a **human flips to ACTIVE**; backend monitors/reports (insights freely readable); material budget/targeting changes route through human approval. **Verdict: ASSISTED — human-in-the-loop required; no autonomous spend in V1.**
